# **Tennis Performance Report**

## **Performance DNA**
- Technical Score: 92/100
- Tactical Score: 88/100

**Summary:** The player demonstrates elite defensive skills, utilizing open-stance sliding on hard courts to neutralize aggressive shots. Footwork patterns are precise, specifically the split-step timing, though court positioning remains passive and deep behind the baseline throughout the rally.

## **Key Strengths**
- Exceptional defensive range and flexibility on the backhand wing, managing a controlled slice while fully stretched (Frames 10-18).
- Textbook split-step timing, initiating the hop exactly as the opponent strikes the ball to maximize reaction speed (Frames 55-60 and 160-165).
- Strong racket preparation on the running forehand, establishing a high unit turn before the feet are fully set (Frames 85-92).

## **Areas to Improve**
- Recovery positioning is too deep behind the baseline, yielding court geometry to the opponent (Frames 40-50).
- Slight loss of center of gravity control during the extreme slide recovery, causing a split-second delay in changing direction (Frames 19-22).
- Passive stance during recovery phase; legs straighten too much between shots rather than maintaining a loaded athletic base (Frames 120-130).

## **Training Plan**

**End-Range Stability Slides**
Practice wide defensive balls requiring a slide into the shot. Focus on engaging the core to arrest momentum immediately after contact (like Frame 15) to shorten recovery time.

**Baseline Tolerance Drill**
A rallying drill where the player is penalized for recovering further than 1 meter behind the baseline. This encourages holding ground rather than retreating deep (fixing the issue in Frames 40-50).

**Heavy Ball Counter-Punching**
Feed wide, heavy forehands requiring the player to hit on the run (as seen in Frame 95). Focus on a high-loop follow-through to neutralize the point rather than attempting flat winners from defensive positions.
